    Panel Session
    WASP @ IJCNLP-AACL 2026

    PanelEasier to Write than to Verify? Generative AI and the Crisis in Scholarly Publishing

    Generative models are now part of every stage of scholarly publishing. Authors use them to draft text, analyze data and produce figures; reviewers use them, often without disclosure, to summarize and assess manuscripts; and publishers and indexing services use them to screen submissions and extract knowledge from the literature. The result is a system under strain. Submission volumes are growing faster than the pool of willing referees, AI-assisted paper mills are producing work that is hard to detect, and the cost of producing a plausible paper has dropped much faster than the cost of checking one.

    This panel brings together researchers and learned societies to discuss how the scholarly record can remain trustworthy under these conditions. Topics include the use of AI in peer review and how it should be disclosed and evaluated; methods for detecting fabricated or low-quality submissions, and their limits; whether papers should become more machine-verifiable, with claims explicitly linked to the data and code behind them; and the effect of AI-mediated discovery on what gets read and cited. We will also consider how current publishing models, designed for human readers, can adapt to a literature increasingly read by machines. The goal is not to settle these questions but to identify where research from the NLP and AI community can make the most difference.
